# What vidsmith Does

## The pitch
[visual: person typing on a laptop in a quiet room at night]
You write a script in a markdown file. vidsmith speaks it, finds footage for every scene, burns captions that land on the word being said, and hands back a finished video.

## It runs on your hardware
[visual: desktop computer running in a home office]
The render happens on your own machine. There is no charge per video, no credits to buy, and no monthly bill that keeps running long after you stopped using it.

## The timings are exact
[diagram: where caption timing comes from]
Most tools transcribe the audio back to find out where the words landed. vidsmith takes the timings from the speech engine that made the sound, so the captions cannot drift and the picture cuts where the speaker actually lands a full stop.

## It reads its own output
[visual: close up of code on a monitor at night]
Before you publish, it checks the delivered files against each other. A thumbnail with the wrong shape, captions running past the end, a credit that never reached the description. Every one of those shipped once, so now it looks.

## Attribution is built in
[visual: photographer holding a camera outdoors]
Stock libraries require you to name the creator and link back. vidsmith builds that block from what the search actually returned and folds it into the description, because it is a licence condition rather than a courtesy.

## What it costs
[diagram: one payment against a monthly subscription]
Free for personal, study and non profit use. Commercial use is a one off licence rather than a subscription. You pay once and there is nothing to renew.

## Where to get it
[visual: hands closing a laptop on a wooden desk]
The source is public on GitHub. Read it, run it, and buy a licence only once it starts making you money.
