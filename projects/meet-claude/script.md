# Meet Claude, Anthropic's AI Assistant

## Lost In Translation
[visual: person staring at glowing screen]
You have spent hours trying to make a machine understand what you actually mean, only to get back a wall of polished filler that sounds like a robot wrote it. The hidden cost of that frustration is the quiet erosion of your trust in every new tool that crosses your desk.

## Another Chatbot Myth
[visual: someone closing a laptop lid]
You might think Claude is just another chatbot that churns out generic email replies. It is a large language model made by Anthropic, shaped by an approach the company calls Constitutional AI. The aim is an assistant that is helpful, honest and harmless. It should reason through a hard question, say when it is unsure, and decline requests that could cause real harm.

## The Constitutional Core
[visual: person reading a thick book]
Anthropic trained this assistant against a set of written principles rather than relying only on people rating its answers. During training, the model critiqued and revised its own responses using that constitution, so those values are built in long before you type your first question.

## Writing And Code
[visual: hands typing on a keyboard]
That shows up in dense writing and complex software work, where it keeps the thread of a long conversation. You can paste thousands of lines of source code or a sprawling manuscript into a single chat and ask for precise edits. Developers can also run Claude Code in a terminal, where it reads a whole project and makes changes across many files.

## Artifacts In Action
[visual: person tapping a tablet screen]
Instead of burying results in a long chat stream, Claude can place a document, a chart or a small web page into a side panel called an artifact. You can refine it and preview it without cluttering the conversation. The same assistant runs in apps for the web, desktop and phone, and businesses build on it through an API.

## The Cost Of Ambiguity
[visual: person rubbing tired eyes]
When you treat the assistant like a magic oracle instead of a diligent junior colleague, you risk publishing unverified facts or missing subtle logic gaps in your projects. Trusting the output without checking the underlying assumptions will eventually lead to embarrassing mistakes in your professional work.

## Better Prompts
[visual: pen writing on paper notebook]
Give it explicit context, clear boundaries and a defined role before asking it to tackle your hardest problems. That small shift turns a clever toy into a reliable partner for your daily routine. Forget those boundaries, and it will simply guess your intent, which usually brings you back to generic answers that need heavy editing to become useful.
