# Predicate Logic Makes Computers Think

## Everyday assumptions fail
[visual: person typing at keyboard]
You have probably searched for flights by typing a phrase into a box and hoping the machine just gets what you mean. That loose approach works fine until a vague filter misses the one cheap ticket you actually needed, costing you hundreds of dollars on your summer vacation.

## Language hides structure
[visual: handwritten notes on paper]
Most people assume standard search bars understand grammar because they handle human sentences with surprising ease. They do not. Natural language is messy, full of hidden ambiguities, and computers cannot process poetry without breaking into total confusion.

## Objects and predicates
[visual: wooden blocks on table]
Predicate logic fixes this gap by splitting every sentence into two distinct pieces. You have constant nouns representing concrete objects, and you have predicates acting as properties or relationships. This turns a fuzzy thought into a sharp statement about the world.

[diagram: a root node branching down to leaf nodes]
Variables step in when you want to talk about whole groups instead of just one single item. You add quantifiers to state whether your rule applies to everything in the room or just to at least one specific object sitting on the desk.

[diagram: boolean true and false gates]
Truth values lock the final system into place by evaluating every combined statement down to a definitive yes or no. You chain these pieces together with logical connectives, building massive rule trees that leave zero room for computer error.

## Database queries break
[visual: frustrated office worker]
When you write sloppy queries for a massive customer database, the software pulls thousands of irrelevant records because it cannot parse your loose phrasing. That mistake floods your server with garbage data, crashing the application right when traffic peaks.

## Formalize your rules
[visual: whiteboard with flowcharts]
Translate your vague requirements into strict predicate statements before you write a single line of code. Define your constants clearly, bind your variables with the right quantifiers, and test every logical branch against edge cases.

## Logic rules everything
[visual: server racks blinking]
Master the predicate, and you will finally speak the native tongue of computation.
