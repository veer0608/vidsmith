# What Happens After The Render

## The hook
[visual: person uploading a video on a laptop at night]
Your video has finished rendering. That is the moment most tools hand it over and stop, and it is the moment almost everything still goes wrong.

## The description that lies
[visual: photographer holding a camera outdoors]
Stock libraries ask you to name the photographer and link back to them. If the text you paste names photographers whose clips are not in the cut you uploaded, you have credited the wrong people in public.

## Chapters that vanish
[visual: scrubbing a video timeline on a laptop]
YouTube drops a whole chapter list rather than the bad line in it. One chapter shorter than ten seconds and the video has no chapters at all, with no error anywhere to tell you.

## The thumbnail from the wrong cut
[visual: phone and a laptop on a desk showing video]
A vertical cut and a wide cut are two different files. Take the thumbnail from the wrong one and you publish the right video wearing the wrong shape.

## So it reads its own output
[visual: person comparing two documents at a desk]
vidsmith opens the files it just delivered and compares them against each other. Every credit against the description that carries it, every caption against the runtime it belongs to, every thumbnail against the cut it names.

## And it checks the live page
[visual: person watching a video on a phone]
Point it at something already published and it reads the public watch page. It will tell you the description never saved, the tags were lost, or the only subtitles up there are YouTube guessing rather than the timings you rendered.

## Where to get it
