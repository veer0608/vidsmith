# Why Your Captions Are Always Slightly Off

## The hook
[visual: person watching a video on a laptop late at night]
You have seen it a hundred times without naming it. The caption appears a beat before the word, or hangs on screen after the sentence has ended. Nobody complains, and everybody notices.

## Why it happens
[visual: sound wave on a studio monitor]
Almost every captioning tool listens to the finished audio and guesses where the words fell. That guess is good. It is not exact, and small errors land on every line of a ten minute video.

## The information already exists
[diagram: where caption timing comes from]
Here is the part that is strange. Something already knew the answer. The engine that spoke the words reported when each one started and stopped, and then the tool threw that away and listened to its own output instead.

## What vidsmith does instead
[visual: close up of code on a monitor at night]
vidsmith keeps it. The speech engine reports a boundary for every word it speaks, and those numbers become the captions. Nothing transcribes anything, so there is no guess to be slightly wrong.

## The same numbers cut the picture
[visual: hands editing video on a computer screen]
Those timings do more than caption. The edit uses them too, so a shot changes where the speaker actually lands a full stop, rather than on a round number somebody picked.

## What you get back
[visual: files on a desk beside a laptop]
Every render hands you the video, a subtitle file for YouTube, and a WebVTT file for anywhere else. You upload them. Nothing is regenerated, and nothing drifts between the two.
